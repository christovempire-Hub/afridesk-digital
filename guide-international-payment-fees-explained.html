International Payment Fees Explained: What Actually Reduces Your Money?

A client can send one amount while a smaller amount reaches you. Understanding where the difference comes from helps you price projects properly and choose payment methods more intelligently.

The four costs to watch

International payments can involve several different costs. The exact combination depends on the service, currency, transaction and withdrawal route.

  • Transaction fee: a percentage or fixed charge for processing the payment.
  • Currency conversion cost: the difference created when one currency is converted into another.
  • Withdrawal or transfer fee: a charge for moving funds from a payment account to another account or wallet.
  • Receiving or bank cost: a charge that may occur when funds reach a bank or other financial account.
Key idea: Never judge a payment option by one advertised fee. Compare the entire path from the client's payment to the money you can actually use.

Why the exchange rate matters

Even when a service advertises a low transaction fee, the conversion rate can materially affect the final amount you receive. If your client pays in a foreign currency but your expenses are in local currency, the conversion result matters just as much as the visible payment fee.

When comparing options, record both the stated exchange rate and the final amount you would receive after conversion and other charges.

A simple way to calculate your take-home amount

Amount available to you = Client payment − transaction fees − conversion costs − withdrawal/receiving costs

This is an estimate rather than a universal accounting formula. The exact charges depend on the payment route you use.

Example

Imagine a client sends an amount worth ₦500,000. After all applicable payment, conversion and withdrawal costs, suppose the amount available to you is ₦472,000.

Your total cost is ₦28,000 and your effective cost is 5.6% of the amount sent.

The important lesson is that your real cost is the difference between what the client sends and what you can actually use—not simply the percentage displayed on a payment page.

How fees affect your freelance price

If you know that payment costs regularly reduce your revenue, include that reality when setting your minimum project price. Otherwise, a project can appear profitable before payment but become much less profitable after the money arrives.

Do not confuse client fees with your fees

Some payment systems may charge the sender, the recipient, or both. A client may also have their own bank or platform costs that are separate from the amount you receive.

Before starting a project, agree on the payment amount and currency and clarify any relevant payment costs so there is less room for disagreement later.

Compare payment methods using the final amount

If you have several legitimate payment options available, compare them using the same hypothetical client payment. Record:

  • Amount the client sends
  • Transaction fee
  • Exchange-rate result
  • Withdrawal or transfer cost
  • Final amount available to you
  • Expected settlement time

Keep a payment record

For every international payment, record the date, client, amount sent, currency, fees, conversion result, amount received and any withdrawal cost. Over time, this creates useful evidence about which payment routes work best for your business.

Review your effective payment cost

Your effective payment cost is useful because it lets you compare transactions with different fee structures.

Effective cost rate = Total payment costs ÷ Amount sent × 100

Review this rate periodically. If your costs are consistently higher than expected, investigate whether another suitable payment route could improve your take-home amount.

Protect yourself from suspicious payment requests

Never assume a message or screenshot proves that money has arrived. Verify payments through the relevant account or platform. Be cautious if someone asks you to pay an upfront fee to receive money, refund an unexplained overpayment, or follow an unfamiliar payment link under urgent pressure.

Make your payment costs visible

Calculate fees before quoting, compare currencies, and build payment costs into your freelance pricing process.
