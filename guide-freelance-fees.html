How to Calculate Freelance Platform and Payment Fees

A project can look profitable until platform commissions, payment processing charges, fixed fees and currency conversion reduce what actually reaches you. Here is a simple way to calculate the real cost before you accept a job.

Why fee calculation matters

If a client agrees to pay $500, you do not necessarily earn $500. A freelance marketplace may take a percentage, a payment provider may charge another percentage or fixed amount, and currency conversion can introduce another cost.

The important number is your net amount — what remains after the fees that apply to the transaction.

The four fees to check

1. Platform or marketplace fee

This is a percentage charged by a freelance marketplace or other platform for facilitating the transaction. Always check the current fee rules for the specific platform, contract type and account because rates can differ.

2. Payment processing fee

A payment provider may charge a percentage of the amount received. Some providers also have different rates depending on payment method, country or transaction type.

3. Fixed payment fee

Some payment methods include a flat charge in addition to a percentage. Fixed fees matter especially on smaller invoices because they represent a larger share of the payment.

4. Currency conversion cost

If the client pays in one currency and you receive another, the exchange rate or conversion spread can reduce your final amount. Treat conversion costs as part of the transaction cost rather than ignoring them.

Step 1: Start with the client payment

Write down the gross amount the client will pay. For example, suppose a client pays $500.

Step 2: Calculate percentage fees

For a percentage fee, use:

Percentage fee = Payment × Fee percentage ÷ 100
Example: A 10% platform fee on $500 is $50.

Step 3: Add fixed fees

If the payment also has a $5 fixed charge, your total fees so far are:

$50 percentage fee + $5 fixed fee = $55 total fees

Step 4: Include other transaction costs

If another provider charges 3% processing, that would be $15 on a $500 payment. If currency conversion creates an additional cost, include that too when you can estimate it.

The goal is not to guess a perfect number. The goal is to avoid pretending that a fee exists only when you see it on your final statement.

Step 5: Calculate your net payment

Once you know the applicable fees:

Net payment = Gross payment − Total fees

For example, if total fees are $70:

$500 − $70 = $430 net payment

Step 6: Calculate your effective fee rate

This helps you understand the real percentage cost of a transaction:

Effective fee rate = Total fees ÷ Gross payment × 100

If you paid $70 in total fees on $500, your effective fee rate is 14%.

Do not confuse the client price with your target earnings

This is where many freelancers make a costly mistake. If you need to receive a particular amount after fees, simply charging that amount as the client price may leave you short.

Instead, work backwards from the amount you want to keep. If fees are percentage-based, you can estimate the gross price required to reach your desired net amount. For mixed percentage and fixed fees, use the actual fee structure to check the result.

AfriDesk tip: Build fees into your pricing before sending the proposal. Do not wait until the client has agreed to your price and then discover that the payment method cuts your margin.

Compare payment methods by net amount

The cheapest-looking option is not always the cheapest option. Compare providers using the amount you actually receive after percentage fees, fixed fees and conversion costs.

  1. Record the client's gross payment.
  2. List every percentage fee.
  3. List every fixed fee.
  4. Estimate currency conversion costs where relevant.
  5. Subtract the total cost from the gross payment.
  6. Compare the final net amount across payment options.

Keep a fee record

For recurring freelance work, keep a simple record of the gross amount, fees, currency, net amount and payment method. After a few transactions, you will have a much clearer picture of which payment routes work best for your business.
