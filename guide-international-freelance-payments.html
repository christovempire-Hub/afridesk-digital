How to Receive International Freelance Payments in Africa

Getting paid by an international client is not only about receiving the money. You also need to understand fees, exchange rates, settlement times, withdrawal options, and the risks that can reduce what you actually keep.

1. Start with the client's payment method

Before you begin a project, ask how the client normally pays international freelancers. Depending on the client and your country, options may include bank transfers, payment processors, payment platforms, or digital wallets.

Do not assume that a payment method available to your client is available to you. Check whether you can open and use the service in your country and whether you can actually withdraw the funds.

2. Compare the full cost, not just the advertised fee

A payment can involve several separate costs. Look at the complete path from the client's payment to money you can actually use.

  • Sender fees: charges paid by the client when sending the money.
  • Platform fees: fees charged by a payment or freelance platform.
  • Payment processing fees: charges for processing the transaction.
  • Currency conversion costs: the difference between the market exchange rate and the rate you receive.
  • Withdrawal fees: charges for moving money from a platform or wallet to your bank or other supported destination.
  • Bank or receiving fees: charges that may apply when funds arrive in your account.
Key idea: A service with a low visible transaction fee is not automatically the cheapest. Compare the final amount you receive.

3. Check the exchange rate

If a client pays in USD, EUR, GBP, or another foreign currency but you spend mainly in your local currency, conversion matters.

Compare the rate offered for the transaction with a reasonable market reference. A small difference can become significant when you receive payments every month.

Use AfriDesk's Currency & Fee Calculator to estimate the effect of conversion and other charges.

4. Check how quickly you can access the money

Settlement time can matter as much as the fee. Before choosing a payment method, check:

  • How long the payment normally takes to arrive.
  • Whether weekends or holidays affect settlement.
  • How long withdrawals take.
  • Whether additional verification can delay access to funds.
  • Whether the service has transaction or withdrawal limits that affect your project size.

5. Make sure withdrawal is practical

Receiving a payment is only useful if you can access it. Confirm the available withdrawal methods before relying on a payment service for client work.

Check whether you can withdraw to your bank account or another supported method, what currencies are supported, and what minimums, limits, or additional charges apply.

6. Agree on the currency and payment terms before starting

Your proposal or contract should make the payment arrangement clear. State the agreed project price, currency, payment schedule, and who is responsible for applicable payment fees when appropriate.

For larger projects, consider milestones rather than waiting until the entire project is finished. This can reduce cash-flow pressure and make the payment process easier to manage.

7. Calculate what you will actually keep

Do not price a project based only on the amount shown on the invoice. Estimate your take-home amount after platform, processing, fixed, conversion, and other relevant fees.

AfriDesk's Freelancer Fee Calculator can help you estimate the total fees and effective fee rate before accepting a payment arrangement.

8. Keep a simple payment record

For every international payment, keep a record of:

  • Client name and project.
  • Invoice number.
  • Amount invoiced and currency.
  • Amount received.
  • Payment and conversion fees.
  • Date payment was sent and received.
  • Exchange rate used, where relevant.
  • Withdrawal amount and fees.

Good records help you understand your real profitability and make it easier to reconcile invoices with your bank or payment accounts.

9. Watch for payment scams

International freelance payments create opportunities for scammers to impersonate clients or payment services.

Be cautious when a client:
  • sends a screenshot as “proof” of payment but the money has not arrived;
  • overpays and asks you to refund the difference before the original payment is safely confirmed;
  • asks you to pay a fee directly to “unlock” a payment without independently verifying the request;
  • asks you to send money, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or personal funds as part of receiving your payment;
  • pressures you to move outside a platform's normal payment process without a good reason.

Verify payments inside your bank or payment account rather than relying only on emails, screenshots, or messages from the sender.

10. A simple payment workflow for freelancers

  1. Agree on the price and payment currency.
  2. Confirm the client's preferred payment method.
  3. Check availability in your country.
  4. Compare total fees, exchange rates, settlement time, and withdrawal options.
  5. Include payment terms in your proposal or agreement.
  6. Send a professional invoice.
  7. Confirm the payment has actually arrived.
  8. Record the amount received and all relevant fees.
  9. Review your actual take-home amount when pricing future projects.
