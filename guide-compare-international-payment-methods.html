How to Compare International Payment Methods in Africa

The cheapest-looking payment method is not always the one that leaves you with the most money. Learn how to compare payment options using the factors that actually affect your freelance or remote-work income.

Why payment method matters

When you receive money from an international client, the amount you keep can be different from the amount the client sends. Costs can appear through payment fees, currency conversion, withdrawal charges, receiving fees, or other transaction costs.

For freelancers and remote workers, the right question is not simply “Which payment method is available?” It is “Which option gives me a practical, reliable way to receive the money I need while keeping costs under control?”

1. Start with the client's payment options

First find out what the client can realistically use. A payment option is not useful if the client cannot send money through it, cannot pay in your preferred currency, or cannot meet the required payment process.

Ask for the payment method, sending currency, expected payment amount, and whether the client or recipient is responsible for any transaction costs.

2. Compare the total cost, not one fee

Look at every cost that can reduce your final amount. Depending on the method, this may include:

  • Percentage transaction or platform fees
  • Fixed transaction charges
  • Currency conversion costs or exchange-rate differences
  • Withdrawal fees
  • Receiving or bank charges
  • Other service-specific costs
Important: A payment method with a low visible transaction fee can still be expensive if its exchange rate or withdrawal cost is less favorable.

3. Compare the exchange rate

If the client pays in one currency and you ultimately need another, the exchange rate matters. Two services can receive the same amount but leave you with different local-currency amounts after conversion.

When comparing options, record the amount the client sends, the currency received, the conversion rate used, and the final amount available to you.

4. Check settlement time

Cost is only one part of the decision. Consider how long the money normally takes to become available and whether the timing works for your business.

A slightly more expensive option may sometimes be more practical if it provides a settlement process that fits your client's deadline and your cash-flow needs. Avoid assuming that a particular method will always be instant or always take a fixed number of days.

5. Check how you can withdraw the money

Receiving money is only useful if you can access it in a practical way. Check the available withdrawal or transfer methods, minimums, limits, supported currencies, and associated costs before choosing an option.

Your comparison should end at the point where the money is actually usable for your needs—not merely when a balance appears on a payment account.

7. Calculate your real take-home amount

Suppose a client sends an international payment. Instead of comparing only the advertised fee, calculate what remains after all relevant costs.

Simple comparison: Client payment − transaction costs − conversion costs − withdrawal/receiving costs = estimated amount available to you.

For a quick calculation, use the AfriDesk Currency & Fee Calculator together with the Freelancer Fee Calculator.

8. Agree on payment terms before starting

Payment problems are easier to prevent before a project begins. Your proposal or agreed project terms should make the payment amount, currency, payment schedule, and relevant payment instructions clear.

If a payment method has costs that affect the amount you receive, decide how those costs will be handled before work begins rather than discovering the difference after payment.

9. Keep records of successful payment methods

Once you receive payments successfully, record what happened. Note the amount sent, amount received, fees, conversion result, settlement time, and any problems encountered.

After several transactions, you will have your own evidence about which methods work best for different types of clients and payment sizes.

International payment checklist
✓ Can the client use the method?
✓ What currency will be sent?
✓ What transaction fees apply?
✓ What exchange rate or conversion cost applies?
✓ Are there withdrawal or receiving charges?
✓ How long will settlement normally take?
✓ How will you access the money after receiving it?
✓ What final amount do you expect to keep?

Do not choose based on fees alone

The best payment option depends on the specific transaction. A method that works well for a small payment may not be the best choice for a large payment. A method that is convenient for one client may not be available to another.

Build the habit of comparing the full cost, final amount, reliability, settlement process, and withdrawal practicality before making a decision.

What to do next

Once you know how to compare payment methods, connect that decision to the rest of your freelance workflow. Price the project, account for fees, agree on payment terms, invoice the client, verify the payment, and keep a record.
