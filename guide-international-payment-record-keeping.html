How to Keep Good Records of International Freelance Payments

A simple record-keeping system helps you understand what clients paid, what fees you lost, what exchange rate you received and how much you actually earned.

Why payment records matter

International freelance payments can involve several numbers: the client's payment, transaction fees, currency conversion, withdrawal charges and the amount that finally reaches you. If you only record the final amount, it becomes difficult to understand your real revenue and costs.

Good records help you reconcile invoices, review payment methods, calculate your true earnings and make better pricing decisions.

What to record for every payment

You do not need a complicated accounting system to start. Keep a consistent record for each international payment.

  • Invoice or project reference
  • Client name or client reference
  • Date the payment was sent
  • Amount the client was expected to pay
  • Currency used
  • Payment method
  • Transaction or platform fee
  • Fixed payment fee, if applicable
  • Exchange rate or conversion information
  • Conversion cost or spread, where known
  • Withdrawal or receiving charge
  • Amount actually received
  • Date the money became available
  • Notes about the transaction

Separate the invoice amount from the amount received

These numbers are not always the same. Your invoice may show the agreed client payment while your account receives a lower amount after fees and conversion.

Basic reconciliation: Expected payment − transaction costs − conversion costs − withdrawal or receiving costs = estimated amount available to you.

Keeping both figures lets you see the real cost of receiving money.

Record the exchange rate

When a payment involves currency conversion, record the exchange rate used or the conversion details shown by the payment service or financial institution. This makes later reviews much easier.

For example, if a client pays in USD and you receive the equivalent in another currency, record the original USD amount, the currency received and the final amount credited after applicable charges.

Match payments to invoices

Give each invoice a unique reference and use that reference in your payment records. When a client pays, mark the invoice as paid only after you have verified the payment in the appropriate account or platform.

This reduces confusion when several clients pay around the same time and makes it easier to investigate missing or unexpected amounts.

Review your payment methods regularly

After several transactions, review your records and look for patterns. One method may appear inexpensive until conversion and withdrawal costs are included. Another may cost more per transaction but provide a settlement process that works better for your business.

Use your actual records to compare methods instead of relying only on advertised headline fees.

You can also use the Freelancer Fee Calculator to estimate the impact of different fee structures.

Keep supporting evidence

Where practical, retain the invoice, payment confirmation, transaction reference and relevant account statement or receipt that supports the transaction. Keep records securely and follow any applicable tax, accounting or record-retention requirements in your jurisdiction.

Simple monthly review

  1. Add up international payments received.
  2. Add up transaction, conversion and withdrawal costs.
  3. Compare expected invoice amounts with actual receipts.
  4. Identify unusually high fees or unexpected deductions.
  5. Review which payment methods worked best.
  6. Use the results to improve future pricing and payment terms.

International payment record-keeping checklist

  • ☐ Give every invoice a unique reference.
  • ☐ Record the original payment amount and currency.
  • ☐ Record transaction and other known fees.
  • ☐ Record conversion information when applicable.
  • ☐ Record the final amount received.
  • ☐ Match the payment to the invoice.
  • ☐ Keep supporting receipts or confirmations.
  • ☐ Review payment costs each month.
