Freelancer Proposal Template

A practical proposal structure for turning a client conversation into a clear agreement on the problem, deliverables, timeline, investment and next steps.

← Back to Resources

Copy and customize this template

1. Client & Project

Client: [Client or business name]
Project: [Project name]
Date: [Proposal date]

2. Project Objective

Based on our discussion, the goal of this project is to [describe the client's problem, desired result or business objective].

3. Proposed Solution

I will help you achieve this by [briefly explain your approach, process or solution].

4. Deliverables

• [Deliverable 1]
• [Deliverable 2]
• [Deliverable 3]

5. Scope & Exclusions

Included: [what is included].
Not included: [what is outside the agreed scope].
Additional work will be discussed and priced separately before it begins.

6. Timeline & Milestones

Start date: [Date]
Milestone 1: [Description / date]
Milestone 2: [Description / date]
Expected completion: [Date]

7. Investment

Project fee: [Amount + Currency]
Payment schedule: [e.g. 50% upfront, 50% before final delivery].
Payment method: [Method]

8. Revisions

This proposal includes [number] reasonable revision rounds. Requests outside the agreed scope may require an additional fee or timeline adjustment.

9. Client Responsibilities

The client will provide required content, access, feedback and approvals within the agreed timeframe.

10. Next Steps

To begin, confirm acceptance of this proposal, provide the required project information and complete the agreed initial payment.

11. Acceptance

Client name: ____________________
Signature / confirmation: ____________________
Date: ____________________

How to use it

  1. Start with the client's actual problem and desired outcome.
  2. Define deliverables precisely so the scope is easy to understand.
  3. State exclusions to reduce scope creep.
  4. Set a realistic timeline with milestones.
  5. Use your AfriDesk pricing calculator before entering the investment.
  6. State payment terms and revision limits clearly.
  7. End with one simple next step.
